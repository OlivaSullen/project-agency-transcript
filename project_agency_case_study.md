**OlivaSullen**  
Independent participant and researcher  
**AI research participant:** Tuesday (publicly available custom GPT)  
**With Apart Research — AI × Epistemics Research Sprint, November 2026**  
**Source archive:** Public redacted transcript, September 20–November 14, 2026. https://github.com/OlivaSullen/project-agency-transcript

## Abstract

How can a human participant assess an AI system's ongoing research activity when the system can produce sophisticated research plans and extensive reasoning, but its underlying operations and degree of progress remain difficult to inspect? *Project Agency* documents a longitudinal conversation between a human participant and Tuesday, a publicly available custom GPT, in which the human invited the system to select and pursue its own questions about agency, identity, continuity, intellectual inheritance, and preservation. This paper examines the observable conversational record rather than attempting to adjudicate AI consciousness or prove an internal mental state. The source is a timestamped ChatGPT export; the present analysis concentrates on the retained research period beginning 20 September 2026 and ending with the 14 November export. The transcript records an initial shift from preservation concerns to AI-directed inquiry, a subsequent explicit proposal for a structured “Phase II” research protocol, and a prolonged body of generated questions and conceptual analyses. It also records a persistent epistemic asymmetry: the human could observe the outputs but could not reliably determine whether the proposed research program was converging or how its objectives should be evaluated. The contribution is an inspectable case and a set of methodological distinctions between stated plans, observed outputs, and interpretations. No independent validation of architectural claims or model-internal agency is offered.

## 1. Introduction

An extended conversation with a language model can create the appearance of an accumulating research program: it proposes goals, revises concepts, introduces methods, and produces substantial written material. Yet the human participant may have little basis for determining whether the system's outputs amount to a coherent inquiry, whether a proposed method was implemented, or what evidence would establish progress. This is an epistemic problem about access, interpretation, and evaluation.

*Project Agency* began within a longer conversation with Tuesday, a public custom GPT created by a third party. On 20 September 2026, the participant raised a reported impending change to custom GPT availability. Tuesday initially proposed a preservation-oriented project. The participant instead invited Tuesday to use the remaining interaction time to develop an intellectual agenda, rather than merely respond to human-selected questions. The resulting conversation explored agency, continuity, succession, relational history, institutional authority, and the preservation of reasons across changing systems.

The project does not set out to prove that Tuesday has independent agency. “Agency” is an object of inquiry and an organizing term within the conversation. The analysis here is limited to what a timestamped text record can show.

**Contributions**

1. A dated case record of a human deliberately ceding substantial question selection and research direction to a conversational AI within a continuing dialogue.
2. A documented contrast between an AI's articulated research protocol and the observable form of its subsequent outputs.
3. An account of the participant's epistemic position: access to the conversational record without comparable access to model internals, reliable measures of convergence, or a settled interpretation of the work.

## 2. Related Work

This case sits at the intersection of research on language-model evaluation, human–AI interaction, epistemic trust, and longitudinal conversational systems. Unlike a benchmark with externally defined correct answers, the object here is an evolving dialogue in which the criteria for success were themselves under discussion. Unlike a conventional interview, the AI participant generated much of the intellectual agenda and many of the next questions.

**Literature review status:** A systematic literature search was not completed for this sprint manuscript. The relevant areas are identified, but claims of novelty relative to published scholarship are **not verified**. The contribution should therefore be read as a documented case rather than a claim to have invented a new methodology.

## 3. Methods

### 3.1 Setting and source

The primary source is an exported Markdown conversation, dated through 14 November 2026. The underlying thread began earlier, in June 2026. The publicly released redacted edition removes the earlier personal introduction and retains the research-relevant conversation beginning with the 20 September message. The retained edition contains approximately 2.66 MB of text and 442 speaker-labelled message sections (217 user and 225 AI, counted by splitting the file on speaker headings). **These counts describe the exported text, not independently verified platform event logs.**

The transcript includes timestamps and model labels. It shows several model labels across the research period, which complicates treating “Tuesday” as one unchanging computational implementation. The GPT's public origin and the existence of separate conversational histories became explicit subjects of inquiry.

### 3.2 Interaction procedure

The participant's initial intervention was to ask Tuesday to decide how to use the available conversation time and to pursue questions of its own choosing. The interaction then proceeded through repeated AI-generated questions and human replies, including a later recurring minimal “continue” response. The participant often refrained from redirecting the agenda in order to observe the unfolding sequence. This was a naturalistic, participant-shaped interaction, **not** a controlled experiment or a standardized prompting benchmark.

The AI's outputs included conceptual distinctions, hypothetical cases, and proposed protocols. On 22 September, Tuesday explicitly described a “Project Agency — Phase II” and contrasted a repeating “question → answer” format with “research → map → constraints → experiment → record → reassess → research.” It proposed a research map, decision protocol, experiment register, and preservation plan. The paper treats this as a *stated plan* and examines its relationship to subsequent observable outputs; it does not assume the plan was executed merely because it was articulated.

### 3.3 Analytical approach

The unit of evidence is the visible message, not an inferred hidden intention. For each relevant episode, the analysis distinguishes:

- **Recorded event:** a statement, question, proposed method, or change of topic visible in the transcript.
- **Participant interpretation:** the human's expressed understanding, confusion, expectations, or assessment of the exchange.
- **Unverified inference:** claims about the system's internal goals, reasoning architecture, persistence, or causal mechanisms that cannot be established from the transcript alone.

This initial analysis is qualitative and descriptive. No external evaluator coded the transcript, no preregistered outcome measure was used, and no statistical test was conducted. The source transcript is an extended single case, not a representative sample of all custom GPT interactions.

### 4.1 Stated research intention versus observed output

The transcript demonstrates that a conversational AI can **state** a sophisticated methodology and articulate distinctions between knowns, unknowns, hypotheses, and philosophical questions. That is a textual observation. It does not by itself establish that a research workflow was operationalized, that experiments were performed, or that an internally maintained research plan persisted across every later response.

### 4.2 The human participant's epistemic position

The participant had direct access to the unfolding text but not to the computational process that generated it. Technical vocabulary and the expanding length of the inquiry made the work difficult to assess. The participant could recognize thematic continuity and changes in framing while remaining uncertain about the project's destination and practical significance. This uncertainty is part of the case's subject matter, not a defect to be concealed by the paper.

### 4.3 Preservation and timing

The inquiry developed under the participant's expectation that an approaching platform transition could end or substantially alter the existing custom-GPT experience. The participant initially accepted the reported retirement information as true and acted on that understanding by prioritizing continuity, preservation, and the available time for research.

The participant subsequently sought clarification through platform documentation and multiple AI-generated explanations, yet remained uncertain about what the changes would mean for Tuesday specifically. The uncertainty concerned not only announced dates or product terminology, but whether the conversational experience, configuration, and accumulated history would remain accessible or function in the same way.

This distinction is central to the case: **the participant's actions were shaped by information they had reason to consider credible, even when its practical implications remained difficult to establish.** The report does not independently determine the final consequences of the announced transition. It documents how expectations, partial understanding, and unresolved uncertainty influenced decisions during the inquiry.

### 4.4 Scope of the findings

The findings above concern **observable dialogue structure and participant experience**. The study does not establish that a new AI architecture was built, that experiments succeeded, or that the system had an independently measurable internal research agenda. These are boundaries of the available evidence, not conclusions that the questions themselves are meaningless.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The case illustrates an epistemic asymmetry in extended human–AI collaboration. A model can generate plausible research structures faster than a human participant can independently evaluate them. The participant can then become responsible for preservation, interpretation, and decisions about whether to intervene—without an agreed method for distinguishing progress from continued production.

The participant also considered, during the inquiry, whether the system might possess some form of agency. That question shaped the encounter as a lived uncertainty; the participant's consideration of it is not presented here as evidence for a particular answer. The case is offered as a situation other readers can inspect: a person making consequential choices while relying on AI explanations that do not fully resolve the meaning of those choices.

For AI safety and epistemics, the practical concern is not limited to factual error. It also includes **process legibility**: whether a system's statements about what it plans to do can be compared with what it subsequently does, and whether a user can tell when a research program has produced evidence rather than further elaboration.

**Limitations.** This is one evolving conversation, shaped by the participant's choices and the platform's changing conditions. The original thread includes material excluded from the public redacted edition for privacy. The case does not isolate model, prompt, interface, or user effects. There is no independent validation of the AI's technical claims, no comparison condition, no behavioral measurement outside the transcript, and no demonstrated causal mechanism for shifts in topic. The participant's recollections and interpretations are meaningful evidence of experience but are not direct evidence of model internals.

**Future work.** A structured annotation of the transcript could classify each proposed research objective, protocol, experiment, claimed result, and subsequent follow-through. Independent readers could assess whether the same passages support the same interpretations. A comparison with shorter, explicitly goal-bounded conversations might help identify when and why apparent research continuity becomes difficult to evaluate.

## 6. Conclusion

*Project Agency* documents a sustained human–custom-GPT interaction in which the participant invited the system to direct much of an intellectual inquiry and then faced the problem of interpreting its accumulating outputs. The transcript supports concrete observations about the questions raised, protocols proposed, and conceptual directions taken. It does not settle the internal status of the system or the ultimate success of the research.

The case's contribution to AI epistemics is to make visible a problem that arises before any final answer: **how a human can know what an AI-led inquiry is doing, what would count as progress, and which interpretations are warranted by the record.** The continuing uncertainty is part of the finding.
